# Harry The Blaze — 5-Minute Starter Story Script (Tinglish)

**Shape:** founder interview, not a product tour. Background → problem → first version → first users → what broke → the insight → what it is now → money → close.
**Runtime:** 5:00–5:15. Product first appears at 4:15 — everything before that has to earn it.
**Where it lives:** YouTube, LinkedIn, website hero. Not a feed ad — that's the 0:30 cut.
**Voice:** the founder, talking to one student across a table. ~150 wpm. Pauses are part of the script.
**Supers stay English.** Tinglish voice, English text.

---

## Why this is the long version

A 5-minute starter story isn't a promo stretched out. It's the format where the *story* is the pitch and the product is the payoff. The audience stays because they recognise their own final year in the first 40 seconds — they're not waiting for a feature list.

The three beats that make or break it:

1. **The failure** (~2:40). If the first version worked on day one, there's no story. The audience is judging whether you actually built this or whether you're selling.
2. **The unfair detail** (~3:15). The friend who said "idi game la undi, real round la ledu" — that's the moment the product got real, and it's more persuasive than any feature.
3. **The two friends** (~3:50). Identical prep, different outcomes. That's the entire thesis for Connect 1:1, and it can't be argued, only told.

Everything else is scaffolding.

## Script

### 1. Cold open — 0:00–0:20

**VO:**
> Rendu vandala mandi. Oka hall. Okate company.
> First round start ayyindi — appude ardham ayyindi, memu prepare chesindi antha waste ani.
> Nenu aa roju build cheyyadam start cheyyaledu. Kani aa confusion ne naa next one year ni decide chesindi.

**On-screen:** `FINAL YEAR.` / `ONE HALL.` / `ONE COMPANY.`
**Visual:** The hall, wide, from the back. Let it sit uncomfortably long. No music yet — room tone only.

---

### 2. Who I am — 0:20–1:00

**VO:**
> Nenu [BRANCH] chadiva, [COLLEGE] lo. Naa batch lo [N] mandi. Placement season lo andariki okate tension — oka company, oka date, oka round.
> Nenu topper ni kaadu. Coding lo bane unna, aptitude lo average. So nenu entrance exam la prepare cheddam ani anukunna. Practice sets, PDFs, roju konni questions.
> Adi correct approach ani naaku appudu anipinchindi. Adi trap.

**On-screen:** `[YEAR] · [COLLEGE]` then `NOT A TOPPER.` → `AVERAGE AT APTITUDE.`
**Visual:** Hostel room, books, a laptop with a half-finished PDF. Ordinary, not cinematic. The point is that it looks like the viewer's room.

---

### 3. The round nobody explained — 1:00–1:40

**VO:**
> Kani aa round entrance exam la ledu. Adi games.
> Balloon math — timer tick avuthu, meeru fast ga calculate cheyyali. Matrix flow — pattern chupistaru, adi maaruthundi, meeru next step cheppali. Hidden maze — screen lo kanipinchadu, meeru gurthu pettukuni navigate cheyyali.
> Ivi memory meeda, speed meeda, pressure meeda. Rote learning tho raadu.
> Inka aa taruvata communication round — read aloud cheyyali, sentence repeat cheyyali, mistake pattukovali, padi nimishallo email rayali.
> Ee rendu rounds gurinchi online lo oka video kuda sarigga ledu.

**On-screen:** `BALLOON MATH` · `MATRIX FLOW` · `HIDDEN MAZE` · `READ ALOUD` · `REPEAT` · `ERROR CORRECTION` · `WRITTEN EMAIL`
**Visual:** Hall footage intercut with real captures of each round. First product appearance — and it's framed as *the exam*, not as a product. No UI chrome, no mouse cursor yet.

---

### 4. The wrong industry — 1:40–2:20

**VO:**
> Nenu chusina material antha — aptitude PDFs, forum lo dhigina HR questions, moodu years back pass aina seniors videos.
> Problem enti ante, aa seniors process ni chepparu, current process ni kaadu. Anduke coaching kuda full ga pani cheyyadu — valla daggara old papers unnayi, kani round design maaruthundi.
> Naa daggara answer ledu. So build cheddam ani decide chesa.
> Naa daggara time undi — [N] rojulu. Money ledu. Coding telusu. Android kaadu, web app — enduku ante naa friends andaru phone lo browser ne open chestharu.

**On-screen:** `OLD PAPERS. NEW ROUND.`
**Visual:** Scroll through the PDFs and the forum threads. Then a hard cut to a blank code editor at 2am. The emptiness is the joke.

---

### 5. What broke — 2:20–3:05

**VO:**
> First version chala simple. Okka game. Timer. Score screen.
> Naa hostel room lo, raatri rendu gantalaki, naa friends ni pilichi test chesa.
> [WHAT BROKE — write your real failure here: "Timer run avuthundi, kani game start avvakamundhe score pampisthundi." / "Speaking round lo mic capture avvatledu — recording silent ga save avuthundi." / "Timer screen pakkana undi, anduke evaru chudaledu, anduke game asalu pressure la anipinchaledu."]
> Adi nenu gamaninchaledu. Naa friend gamaninchadu.
> Aa moment lo naaku ardham ayyindi — naa actual problem build cheyyadam kaadu. Naa friends adi vadatledu. So nenu build cheyyadam aapesi, valla pakkana kurchoni chusa.

**On-screen:** `v1`
**Visual:** The worst-looking version. Broken UI, console errors, a score of 0. Do not clean this up — the roughness is the credibility.

---

### 6. First users — 3:05–3:40

**VO:**
> Naa batch lo [N] mandi use chesaru. Andulo [N] mandi actual ga round attend ayyaru.
> Valla feedback brutal ga undi. "Idi game la undi, kani real round la ledu" annaru.
> Enduku? Real round lo timer meeru chudaru. Adi corner lo untundi, meeru daanini marchipovali. Naa version lo timer centre lo undi — adi game la chesindi.
> So timer ni corner ki pampinchanu, chinnaga chesanu. Chinna maarpu. Kani lesson peddadi: realism, features kaadu.

**On-screen:** `"IDI GAME LA UNDI."` (quote card, no name, no photo)
**Visual:** Side-by-side of the two timer positions. One visual argument, five seconds. This is the shot that proves the product was built from feedback.

---

### 7. The two friends — 3:40–4:15

**VO:**
> Kani oka problem undi, adi nenu build chesi solve cheyyalevu.
> Naa friend round clear ayyadu. Inko friend clear avvaledu. Iddari prep same — same games, same practice scores, same material.
> Difference enti?
> Oka manishi aa room lo kurchoni em feel ayyado, aa tension ela handle chesado — adi practice lo raadu. Evaru chepparu.
> So nenu practice ni aapaledu. Adi chalu kaadu ani oppukoni, aa second half ki oka manishi ni add chesa.

**On-screen:** `SAME PREP.` → `DIFFERENT RESULT.`
**Visual:** Two score screens, identical numbers. Hold until it's uncomfortable. Then the laptop closes.

---

### 8. The reveal — 4:15–4:45

**VO:**
> Idi Harry The Blaze.
> Modati bhagam — practice. Accenture 2026 cognitive games inka communication rounds, mee time lo, mee phone lo. Games lo levels unnayi, speaking round mee voice ni score chestundi, ekkada aagipoyaro chepthundi.
> Rendu — Connect 1:1. Iravai nimishalu, oka senior tho, one-to-one. Aa senior ee year ne place ayyaru.
> Meeru adagalsina questions asalu adagandi. "Round lo em adigaru." "Resume ela chusaru." "Nenu ekkada fail avuthanu."

**On-screen:** wordmark → `PRACTICE` / `CONNECT 1:1` → three real questions
**Visual:** First time the real product appears clean and full-screen. The three questions land as type, one after another. This is the only place in the film with polish.

---

### 9. Money — 4:45–5:05

**VO:**
> Money gurinchi clear ga chepthanu, enduku ante meeru student, mee budget chinnadi.
> Practice premium ₹299. Oka saari. Games, levels, full communication rounds.
> Connect separate — booking ki pay chestaru, senior price fix chestaru. Memu slot confirm chesi, iddariki okate meeting link pampisthamu. Paisal gurinchi em daachaledu.

**On-screen:** `₹299` → `CONNECT — PAID PER BOOKING` → `ONE LINK. IDDARIKI.`
**Visual:** Calm, still frame. No animation. Price in silence — kill the music under this block.

---

### 10. Close — 5:05–5:20

**VO:**
> Nenu cheppagaliginadi ide.
> Round ni nerchukovachu. Kani aa room lo ela feel avuthundo nerchukolemu.
> So rendu cheyyandi. Practice cheyandi. Taruvata just place ayina manishi tho matladandi.

**On-screen:** `harrytheblaze.site` · `Free account. Premium ₹299.`
**Visual:** Return to the hall from the cold open, same angle, now empty. Full circle, no voiceover over it — let the last line land in silence.

## What I need from you

Six fills and the film is real. Without them it's a well-built shell.

1. **`[BRANCH]` `[COLLEGE]` `[YEAR]`** — real, or cut the scene to "final year, [CITY]".
2. **`[N]` in the hall** — the actual number. If you don't know, say "almost the whole batch."
3. **The build failure at 2:20** — the single most important line in the script. One specific bug, in one sentence. Vague is fatal here.
4. **`[N]` used it, `[N]` sat the round** — real numbers, however small. "Four friends" is stronger than a fake 200.
5. **The friend's feedback at 3:05** — the exact sentence they said. Keep it rude.
6. **The two friends at 3:40** — did this actually happen, or is it a composite? If composite, say "two people in my batch" and keep it honest.

**Rules that don't move:** no placement rate, no "thousands of students", no Accenture partnership, no testimonial quote with a name and photo. There's no approved evidence for any of it, and `PRODUCT.md` rules it out. A 5-minute film with six specific true details beats a polished one with invented numbers — that's the whole format.

## Delivery notes (Tinglish)

**Code-switch, don't translate.** `timer`, `score`, `matrix`, `communication round`, `cognitive`, `read aloud`, `meeting link`, `slot confirm`, `premium` stay English. The Telugu carries the feeling; the English carries the machinery. Translating these makes it sound dubbed.

**Two or three Telugu words per line.** Full Telugu sentences turn a founder story into a regional ad.

**Three deliberate silences.** After "Adi trap." (1:00) · after "Evaru chepparu." (4:10) · after the last line. Each one is 1.5–2s of nothing. They're what separates a story from a pitch, and they're the first thing an editor will cut if you don't protect them.

**Record a human.** Telugu-English code-switching is mangled by every TTS engine — different accent on each language, sounds translated. This needs the founder's own voice.

**B-roll you can't fake:** the hall, the hostel room, the 2am laptop, the two score screens, the empty hall at the end. Everything else can be screen capture.
